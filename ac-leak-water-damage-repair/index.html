When your air conditioner is the source of the leak

AC leak water damage repair in Mission Viejo, CA means drying and rebuilding the parts of a house that got wet from an air conditioner, furnace coil or air handler, not fixing the machine. Your HVAC company handles the unit and drain line. We handle the wet insulation, drywall, framing and flooring the water reached.

If the unit is still dripping, call the HVAC tech first. If a ceiling is stained or the closet carpet squishes, call us as well, because water that left the pan keeps spreading after the unit is repaired.

How a cooling system ends up soaking a house

Every air conditioner pulls moisture out of the air as it cools. That water collects on the evaporator coil, drops into a pan and leaves through a condensate drain line, usually a small PVC pipe. Algae and sludge build up in that pipe, and once it clogs the pan fills and spills. Many Southern California homes put the air handler in the attic or a hallway closet, so the spill lands on insulation and ceiling drywall.

Summer makes it worse. According to National Weather Service and NOAA climate summaries, the region gets very little rain from June through September, so the coil runs for hours every day and makes the most condensate of the year. A line that coped in March can clog by August.

Signs the water is coming from your HVAC system

Brown stain under an attic unit

A ceiling spot that grows after hot days and shrinks on cool ones points to condensate, not the roof.

Water or rust at the closet unit

A damp closet floor, rusty pan or water line on the base trim means the pan has been overflowing for a while.

Musty smell near vents or the closet

Odor without a visible puddle usually means wet insulation or drywall hidden behind the surface.

Soft or bubbling drywall

Paint that blisters or a ceiling that sags a little between joists is holding water. Press gently, do not poke.

Safety first: the first hour with a leaking AC

Water and electricity share a ceiling in many homes, so take these steps in order and skip any you cannot do safely.

1

Switch the system off

Set the thermostat to Off. If you can reach the breaker without standing in water, turn it off too. If the floor around the panel is wet, do not touch it.

2

Watch for a sagging ceiling

A bulging, dripping or dark ceiling can drop without warning. Keep people and pets out from under it and put a bucket where it drips. Do not poke the bulge from below.

3

Stop more water from arriving

Soak up standing water with towels or a wet-vac and move valuables off wet carpet. Do not run the AC again to see if the leak stopped.

4

Make the two calls

Call your HVAC company to fix the unit and clear the line. Then call our 24/7 line to dry and assess the structure. If you smell gas or see sparks, leave and call 911 before anything else.

How the crew dries and repairs an AC leak

The drying side follows the same logic as any leak, and our water mitigation page covers it in depth. What is different here is where the water hides: above a ceiling, inside insulation and along top plates, where you cannot see it.

1

Map the wet area

Moisture meters and a thermal imaging camera trace the water from the stain to its edges. Attic insulation is checked by hand because it holds water like a sponge.

2

Remove what cannot dry

Soaked insulation and ceiling drywall that has sagged or softened come out. Small openings let us dry the cavity behind. Dry, sound materials stay.

3

Dry the structure

Air movers and LGR dehumidifiers run until the framing and remaining drywall read dry on the meters, usually several days. Readings are logged each visit.

4

Repair and finish

Insulation is replaced, drywall is patched, taped, textured and primed, and paint is matched as closely as the existing finish allows. Scope and price are explained before work starts.

Slow drip or sudden overflow: why it matters for insurance

Insurers tend to separate sudden and accidental water damage from damage that built up over time. A pan that cracked overnight and flooded a closet is often treated as sudden. A clogged line that dripped into a ceiling for months is often treated as gradual or maintenance, and gradual damage is commonly excluded.

That is general, not a ruling on your policy. Read it and ask your insurer how it treats HVAC-related water. Photograph the stain, the pan and the drain line before anything changes, and keep a log of calls and emails.

According to the California Department of Insurance, a policy may not cover further damage if you did not take reasonable steps to protect the property. Turning the unit off and starting drying promptly is that kind of step. Flood coverage is a separate matter and does not apply to an AC leak inside the house.

When moisture from the unit feeds mold

Mold needs moisture, and a slow AC leak supplies it for weeks in a warm, dark attic or closet. Dark spots on a ceiling, fuzzy growth on insulation or a musty smell that lingers after drying suggest the moisture sat there a while. Fix the water source first, then deal with growth.

Our mold remediation page explains containment, HEPA air scrubbers and independent clearance testing. We do not diagnose illness or make health claims. Health questions belong to a physician.

Mistakes that make AC leaks worse

  • Running the system again to see if the leak is gone, which refills the pan.
  • Pouring bleach down the drain line and calling it fixed, with no check of the pan or ceiling.
  • Leaving wet insulation in place because the surface looks dry.
  • Skipping a secondary pan float switch, which shuts the unit off when the pan fills.

A secondary pan and a float switch cost little next to a replaced ceiling. Ask your HVAC company about both, and have the line cleared each spring.

AC leak questions from Mission Viejo homeowners

Is emergency AC leak water damage repair available in Mission Viejo?

Yes. Our line is open 24/7, with same-day response when available and no promised arrival time. Turn the system off at the thermostat first, keep people clear of a sagging ceiling and call your HVAC company for the unit itself.

Does my HVAC technician fix the water damage too?

Usually not. A technician repairs the unit and clears the drain line. Wet insulation, drywall and framing are a separate drying and rebuild job, which is where we come in.

How do I know if my ceiling stain is an AC leak or a roof leak?

Stains from an attic air handler grow on hot days and with heavy cooling, even when it has not rained. Roof leaks follow storms. If you cannot tell, our crew can check with a moisture meter.

Will insurance pay for an AC leak?

It depends on your policy. A sudden overflow is often treated differently from a slow drip that went on for months, and gradual damage is commonly excluded. Read your policy and ask your insurer, and do not assume approval.

My closet floor is wet and the room smells musty. What should I do?

Switch the AC off, soak up standing water and call both your HVAC company and our 24/7 line. A musty smell often means water inside the wall or floor, and drying quickly helps limit mold growth.

How long does drying take after an AC leak?

A small closet can take a few days, and a ceiling with wet framing longer. We follow moisture readings, not a calendar, and you can ask any company whether its crews work to ANSI/IICRC S500.
