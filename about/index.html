About Mission Viejo Water Damage Pros

We are a 24/7 dispatch line, not a single repair crew. Here is how the line works and what to ask before anyone starts work in your home.

A dispatch line for Mission Viejo homeowners

Mission Viejo Water Damage Pros is a 24/7 dispatch line. When you call, we connect homes in Mission Viejo, CA and nearby cities with licensed and insured local restoration contractors, and those contractors do the physical work. We are not one company with one fleet of trucks, and we would rather say so up front.

Why run it this way? Water does not keep office hours. A supply line splits at 2 a.m., a washer hose lets go on a Sunday, a roof leaks in the first winter storm. One line that answers around the clock, collects the basic facts and hands the job to a contractor who can take it beats working down a list of search results with a wet floor underfoot.

We say same-day response when available, and we mean exactly that. Nobody on this line can promise an arrival time, because it depends on the contractor's schedule, traffic and how many homes are wet that day.

What we do and what we do not do

On the call we ask about the water source, the rooms affected, whether the power is on and whether you have stopped the leak. Then we pass the job to a contractor from our network. That contractor handles emergency water removal, structural drying, water damage cleanup and, when moisture has already fed growth, mold remediation. The full list sits on the services page.

  • We do not rent equipment. Air movers and dehumidifiers only go into a home with a crew that sets them, checks them and removes them.
  • We do not repair plumbing. A plumber fixes the pipe. Restoration dries and cleans what the leak soaked.
  • We do not do waterproofing. Sealing a foundation or regrading a yard is separate work, and a drying crew is not the right call for it.
  • We do not handle fire damage.

How scope and price are explained

Nobody can price water damage over the phone, and we will not pretend to. The contractor who takes your job looks at the property first, uses moisture meters and a thermal imaging camera to find wet material behind surfaces, and then walks you through what they found.

Scope and price are explained before work starts. That means you hear which water category applies, how much of the home is affected, which materials can be dried and which must come out, how many days of drying are likely and what equipment will run. If the plan changes after demolition, the contractor should tell you why before more work is done.

Insurance is a separate conversation with your insurer. Sudden and accidental water damage is often covered under a homeowners policy, while gradual leaks and flood usually are not. Read your policy and ask your insurer, because we cannot tell you what it will pay.

Standards: ask whether crews work to them

Two published industry standards describe how this work should be done. ANSI/IICRC S500 covers professional water damage restoration, and ANSI/IICRC S520 covers professional mold remediation. They are public documents, and they are a fair yardstick for any company you consider.

Ask any company whether its crews work to them. Then ask what that looks like on your job: daily moisture readings written down, drying goals set before equipment goes in, and a clear reason for each material that is removed instead of dried.

What to ask any contractor before you sign

This applies to the contractors we dispatch and to anyone else you might hire. The California Contractors State License Board (CSLB) lets you look up a licence number online, and it takes a few minutes.

  • Ask for the licence number and check it on the CSLB website. A written home improvement contract above a small dollar amount must show the licence number.
  • Ask which licence classification covers the work. CSLB has no separate water damage or mold classification, so the answer tells you how the company is licensed.
  • Know the unlicensed-work limit. State law allows unlicensed work only below a small dollar threshold (check the CSLB site for the current figure), and only when no workers are hired and no building permit is needed.
  • Watch the down payment. California caps it at 10 percent of the contract price or a set dollar amount, whichever is less, and you have a three-day right to cancel a home improvement contract.
  • Be cautious with door-to-door repair offers after a storm, and do not let anyone rush you.
  • Ask for proof of insurance, and ask Mission Viejo Building Services whether your repairs need a permit.

If a company cannot answer these plainly, that tells you something before any equipment shows up.
