Condo Water Damage Insurance in Mission Viejo, CA

A brown ring on your bedroom ceiling and a silent unit upstairs. Before anyone argues about fault, two insurance policies and a set of CCRs decide who does what.

By the Mission Viejo Water Damage Pros team

Two policies, one wet wall

Most condo water losses involve two insurance policies. The association usually carries a master policy on the building. You usually carry an HO-6 policy on your unit, which is the condo version of a homeowners policy. Which one responds depends on where the damage is and what your governing documents say.

No rule fits every building. Some associations insure the bare structure only, others insure the unit as originally built, including cabinets and fixtures. Read your CCRs (the declaration of covenants, conditions and restrictions, often shortened to CCRs) and both policies. Do not rely on a neighbor's memory of how the last claim went. Ask the HOA manager for copies if you do not have them. Associations generally keep them on file and know which pages answer your question.

Walls-in, walls-out: a decision framework

Start with one question: where did the water start, and what did it wet? The table below shows the usual split in general terms. Your documents may draw the line somewhere else, so treat it as a conversation starter with your agent and your HOA manager.

SituationWho is usually first in lineFirst move
Pipe burst inside your wall or in your unitYour HO-6 and the association, depending on where the pipe sits and what the CCRs sayShut your valve, tell your insurer and your manager
Common line or roof leak into your unitAssociation master policy for the building, your HO-6 for your own belongings and improvementsReport to the HOA in writing, photograph everything
Neighbor above leaks into your unitOften their liability coverage and the master policy, plus your HO-6 for your contentsDocument, notify both the HOA and your insurer, do not wait on fault
Your own appliance leaks into the unit belowYour HO-6 liability portion, plus the master policyStop the water, tell your insurer you may be liable
Rising or surface water from outsideNeither standard policy, flood coverage is separateAsk your agent about flood coverage

Notice the pattern: fault is decided later. Mitigation and notice happen first. If you wait for a verdict on whose pipe it was, the drywall keeps absorbing.

When the leak comes from the unit above

Do these in the first hour

Tell the neighbor if they are home, because they can close their own valves. Photograph the ceiling, the walls and the floor. Move furniture and electronics away from the drip, and put a bucket or a plastic sheet where it lands. If the ceiling bulges, stay out from under it. Water pooling in a ceiling is heavy, and a controlled puncture is a job for a crew, not a broom handle.

Notify in writing

Send your HOA manager an email describing what happened, with photos. Most associations expect notice, and a written record shows when you reported it. Send your own insurer the same facts. Keep a log of calls and names, as the California Department of Insurance advises.

Do not wait for the cause

The source in the other unit may take days to find. Your side still needs extraction, air movers and LGR dehumidifiers and moisture checks. Your insurer will expect you to take reasonable steps to limit further damage, so ask what documentation they want to see from the drying work.

What stays wet inside a condo

Condos stack water. A leak in a second-floor unit can travel down a wall cavity into the unit below, then into the ceiling of the garage or the common hallway. Water follows framing, pipe penetrations and electrical chases, so the wet area is often larger than the stain you can see.

Thermal imaging cameras and moisture meters find those hidden paths. A crew will typically check the ceiling above, the walls around the stain and the floor below. Insulation soaked with clean water may be dried or removed depending on its condition. Drywall that has sagged or crumbled comes out. Baseboards, cabinet kicks and flooring edges often need to be lifted to dry the cavity behind them.

Questions to ask before you sign anything

  • Does the master policy cover the unit as built, or the bare walls only?
  • Which fixtures, cabinets and flooring does my HO-6 cover?
  • Does my HO-6 have loss-of-use coverage if I cannot live here?
  • What is my deductible on the HO-6, and does the HOA have one that can be assessed to owners?
  • Does my policy include coverage for water that backs up from drains?
  • Who gets to choose the restoration contractor in a common-area loss?
  • Will the HOA require its own vendor, and can I share my drying logs with them?

Selecting the crew

If your HOA assigns a vendor for common-area work, ask whether that vendor will also dry your unit or whether you need your own. A crew for emergency water removal should explain scope and price before work starts, document moisture readings daily and say clearly what is being removed and why.

Keep photos taken before and after, and ask for the drying log when the equipment comes out. Those records help both your insurer and your association.

If the loss is large, ask your agent whether your HO-6 pays for temporary housing while walls are open, and keep receipts for every night. Also ask whether the association's deductible can be passed to owners after a master policy claim. That answer sits in the governing documents, and it is far easier to learn before the claim than after.

If you live in a building with shared walls, read your association documents before you need them and keep a copy of your HO-6 declarations page where you can find it quickly.

Condo water damage questions

What share of Mission Viejo homes are condos, townhomes or apartments?

Housing data compiled for the city from California Department of Finance estimates puts single-family detached homes at 70.9 percent of units. Attached homes, two to four unit buildings and larger multifamily buildings make up the rest. Which category your building falls into matters less than what your CCRs say about repairs.

Does my HOA's insurance cover my interior walls?

It depends on the association's governing documents and its master policy. Some policies cover the unit as originally built, others only the structure. Read both, and ask your agent to compare them against your HO-6.

My upstairs neighbor caused the leak. Do I file with my own insurer?

Report it to your own insurer and your association promptly. Fault can be sorted out later, and your insurer may coordinate with the other party's coverage. Meanwhile, keep drying your unit.

Should I tell my HOA before I start drying?

Tell them at once, in writing, but do not delay protective steps while you wait for an answer. Extraction and drying limit the damage and are generally what insurers expect.

Can I use my own restoration company in a condo?

For your unit, usually yes, though some associations require approved vendors for work in common areas or that affects building systems. Ask your manager and read the CCRs.
