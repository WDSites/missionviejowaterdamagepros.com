When a storm sends water into a Laguna Niguel home

Laguna Niguel averages about 13.1 inches of rain a year, according to the city's hazard mitigation plan, and it arrives in sporadic, intense storms. A dry autumn lets leaves pile into gutters and area drains. Then one heavy night asks every roof and yard to move months of rain in a few hours. If water is already inside, jump to the checklist below and call the 24/7 line.

The city's General Plan puts the main flood danger somewhere unglamorous: damaged, under-engineered or obstructed infrastructure. A clogged catch basin or a blocked yard drain can do more harm than a creek. Walk your lot before the first storm, clear downspout outlets and area drains, and report a blocked street drain to the city. Clear drains are the cheapest protection a house has.

Once water crosses the threshold, emergency water removal is the first job. Extraction units pull standing water, then drying starts before wet drywall and subfloor turn into a bigger repair.

Creeks, a lake and a canyon road

The Sulphur Creek corridor

The city's hazard plan says Sulphur Creek drains most of northern Laguna Niguel, running through Crown Valley, Crown Valley Park, Laguna Niguel Regional Park and Sulphur Creek Reservoir, also known as Laguna Niguel Lake. Crown Valley Parkway follows it. If your home sits low along that route, check the FEMA flood map for your address.

Salt Creek canyons

Two forks of Salt Creek flow south through the southern half of the city, and Niguel Road runs along the canyon. Water leaving a hill has to go somewhere, so keep drain lines clear and watch the base of walls for dampness after storms.

The Aliso Creek edge

Aliso Creek only brushes the northwest border, where Alicia Parkway follows it. Do not assume creek flooding for the rest of town. Most wet losses start with a roof, a plumbing line or a drain.

If water is still spreading in Laguna Niguel

Stop the source

Shut off the main water valve if a pipe or heater is the cause. If rain is pushing in from the yard or slope, stay out of the flow and block the low door only if you can do it safely.

Power only if safe

Wet outlets, a garage opener or a panel near the water mean do not step in. Turn off the breaker only from a dry spot, and otherwise call your utility or 911.

Lift valuables up

Move rugs, boxes, electronics and papers off wet floors and onto counters. Garage storage on the floor is the usual casualty.

Keep people and pets out

Runoff from a slope or a backed-up sewer line carries soil and bacteria. Keep children and pets away, and do not wade in barefoot.

Call the 24/7 emergency line

If you smell gas, leave and call the gas utility or 911 first. Then call us with the address and what you see, and we dispatch.

Hillside lots: when the water comes from the ground

On a sloped lot, rain that soaks the soil behind a wall or along a slab edge can show up inside as damp baseboards, a musty corner or a floor edge that stays wet days after a storm. It is easy to mistake for a plumbing leak. A plumber's pressure test and our moisture meters together tell the two apart, and the fix for each is different.

The General Plan maps 1 percent annual-chance FEMA flood zones in several locations, with a few northern areas in the 0.2 percent zone and the rest of the city minimal. Check your parcel on the FEMA Map Service Center. The California Department of Insurance says standard homeowners policies typically exclude flood, mudslide and debris flow.

The city's hazard plan notes Laguna Niguel does not participate in the NFIP while using its flood maps, so ask your agent how flood coverage works for your address before a storm.

How a drying job runs

Day one: find it and remove it

Thermal imaging cameras and moisture meters map the wet area, including cavities that look fine. Extraction units pull the water. Soaked pad, baseboards and swollen drywall come out so they dry faster or get replaced.

Days two to several: dry to a number

Air movers and LGR dehumidifiers run while we log moisture readings. Drying ends when materials reach dry readings, not when the room feels dry. Ask any company whether its crews work to ANSI/IICRC S500, the published water damage standard.

Water and sewer: who to call

The city's hazard plan says Moulton Niguel Water District and the South Orange County Wastewater Authority took part in its planning, and it lists the city's water system, wastewater collection system and treatment plants on La Paz Road and Alicia Parkway as key assets. Moulton Niguel's own annex lists Laguna Niguel in its service area. Confirm your district on your bill, since we cannot assume it for your address.

Call the district for a main break in the street or a sewer backup that reaches the curb. Call us for water inside the house. For sewage, stay out until the crew arrives.

Laguna Niguel water damage questions

Is emergency water removal available in Laguna Niguel?

Yes. The emergency line runs 24/7, including nights, weekends and storm days, with same-day response when available. We do not promise an arrival time, but we dispatch once we have your address.

Do you cover every ZIP code in Laguna Niguel?

Yes. Laguna Niguel uses ZIP 92677 for street addresses, from the Crown Valley and Sulphur Creek area in the north to the Salt Creek canyons in the south. Give us your street address when you call.

Which water district serves my Laguna Niguel home?

Check your bill. The city's hazard plan names Moulton Niguel Water District and the South Orange County Wastewater Authority in its planning, but it does not map who serves each street. The district handles the street, we handle the water inside.

My wall base is damp after rain but no pipe is leaking. What do I do?

Photograph it, clear nearby drains and gutters, and call. Moisture meters show whether water is coming from outside or from a line. Restoration dries the damage but is not waterproofing, so a drainage specialist handles the cause outside.

Does insurance cover storm water in my house?

Often not. The California Department of Insurance says standard policies typically exclude flood, mudslide and debris flow. Sudden pipe damage is handled differently. Read your policy and ask your insurer, and see our flood damage restoration page.

Do I need a permit for repairs in Laguna Niguel?

The city's page does not list repair rules, so use the Online Permit Center or ask the city before rebuilding walls or plumbing. We explain the work scope first so you know what to ask.

How long does drying take?

Usually several days, depending on the water category, the affected area and the materials. Hardwood and insulated walls take longer than tile. Our readings decide when drying is done.
