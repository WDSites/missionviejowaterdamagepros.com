Storm water is coming in: start here

Storm water damage in Mission Viejo, CA is what happens when rain gets into the house from outside: under a garage door, around a window frame, through a stucco crack or off a patio that slopes toward the wall. Call the 24/7 line once you are safe. While you wait, stop what you can, lift valuables off the floor and keep kids and pets out of the wet area.

This page covers rain that enters a house. Water that rises from a creek, a street or the ground itself is a different job, and our flood damage restoration page covers it. If the water is coming down through a ceiling from the roof, the roof leak page is the better fit.

How rain finds its way into a Mission Viejo house

Most storm intrusions follow a short list of paths. Wind pushes rain sideways into window frames and under door thresholds. Gutters packed with leaves overflow and soak the wall below. Soil or a concrete patio that slopes toward the house sends runoff to the foundation edge and into the garage.

National Weather Service summaries recorded the February 4 to 8, 2024 storms as hard on Orange County, with 4 to 10 inches of rain. A multi-day soaking like that finds every weak seal, so recheck walls and floors after the sky clears.

What storm water damage looks like

Damp carpet along one wall or door

Water crept in under a threshold or through the wall base. The pad underneath holds moisture long after the surface feels dry.

Bubbling paint or brown rings on drywall

Water is wicking up from the bottom plate or running down inside the wall. The stain is smaller than the wet area.

Wet garage floor with muddy edges

Runoff came under the garage door and carried soil with it. Stored boxes on the floor soak up water fast.

Window sills that stay damp

Wind-driven rain got past the frame or flashing. Check the wall below for soft drywall or swelling trim.

Musty smell days after the rain

Moisture is still trapped in a cavity, pad or baseboard. Mold needs moisture, so find the wet spot early.

Safety in the first hour of a storm leak

Water and electricity do not mix, and a storm often knocks out lights, which hides the hazard. Work through these in order.

  • If water is near the panel, outlets or a ceiling light fixture, stay out of the room and shut off power only if you can reach the panel while standing in dry conditions.
  • Smell gas or see a downed line? Leave, call 911 and the utility, then call us.
  • Do not wade through water that came from the yard or street. It may carry soil, fuel or sewage.
  • Move electronics, documents and furniture legs off wet flooring. Place foil or plastic blocks under sofa legs.
  • Photograph everything before you move it, then call the 24/7 line for same-day response when available.

Why storm water is treated as Category 2 or 3

Rain that falls from the sky is clean. Rain that crosses a roof, a yard, a driveway or a gutter is not. It collects soil, bird droppings, fertilizer, motor oil and bacteria on the way in, so restoration crews treat it as Category 2 (significant contamination) and often Category 3 (grossly contaminated) once it has run across ground.

Category decides what can be dried and what must be removed. ANSI/IICRC S500 is the published reference for water damage work, so ask any company whether its crews follow it. Time matters too: clean water that sits a day or two slides down a category.

Mud and silt need their own plan. Fine silt dries into a film that holds moisture against a wall. We shovel and extract it first, then wash and disinfect the surface before drying starts.

Before the next storm: sandbags, gutters and doorways

The city of Mission Viejo says free sandbags are available around the clock at seven city facilities, including Alicia Park, Gilleran Park and the Civic Center, first come, first served, with a limit of 10 per resident. Pick them up while the sky is still dry, because lines and supplies change fast once rain starts. Put them across garage doors and ground-level doorways, not against the walls.

The same city page tells residents to clear gutters, downspouts and low areas of yards and patios so water does not back up. National Weather Service summaries date Tropical Storm Hilary to August 20 and 21, 2023, and the city reported about 3,500 sandbags handed out with no major damage. Preparation pays off, so do it before the forecast turns.

Gutters and downspouts

Clear leaves and check that downspouts empty well away from the foundation, not onto a patio that slopes toward the house.

Garage door seal

Look at the rubber bottom seal for gaps and cracks. A sandbag across the threshold helps while you replace it.

Patio and yard slope

Water should run away from walls. Soil that has settled against stucco is a common low point.

Window frames and caulk

Press a finger along caulk lines. Cracked or pulled-away caulk is an open door for wind-driven rain.

After the storm: check these five places

Water goes where you are not looking. Walk the house in daylight once the rain eases.

  • Attic: look for damp insulation, dark roof decking or drips below flashing. Roof sources belong on the roof leak page.
  • Ceilings: new brown stains or sagging paint, especially below the attic and near exterior walls.
  • Garage: wet floor edges, damp drywall at the base of the walls and soaked boxes.
  • Lower spaces and closets: check carpet edges, baseboards and crawl or utility spaces if your home has them.
  • Exterior: stucco cracks, window frames, cracked caulk and soil piled against the foundation.

How the crew handles a storm water job

1

Extract and remove silt

Truck or portable extraction units pull standing water. Mud and debris are scooped out before anything is dried.

2

Map and test the moisture

Moisture meters and thermal imaging cameras show how far water traveled behind baseboards and under flooring.

3

Clean, remove and dry

Contaminated pad and soft drywall come out. Surfaces are cleaned and treated, then air movers and LGR dehumidifiers dry the structure.

4

Monitor until dry

We recheck readings daily and stop equipment only when the materials reach dry standards. Scope and price are explained before work starts.

Rebuilding drywall, paint and flooring comes after drying, and water damage repair covers that stage.

Door-to-door repair offers after a storm

After heavy rain, people knock on doors offering quick repairs. The Contractors State License Board warns residents to be careful with door-to-door repair offers after a disaster and to avoid rushing into repairs. Check any contractor's license number on the board's site before you sign.

Under the board's contract rules, the down payment is capped at 10 percent of the price (or a smaller fixed limit), there is a 3-day right to cancel, and the contract must show the license number. If someone wants a large cash deposit on your porch, walk away.

Does insurance cover storm water?

It depends on how the water got in. The California Department of Insurance says standard homeowners policies do not cover flood, and flood claims go through the federal NFIP, which is a separate policy. Rain that enters through a damaged roof or a storm-opened window is often treated differently from groundwater or runoff, so read your policy and ask your insurer.

The department's claims guide also says an insurer may not cover further damage if you did not take reasonable steps to protect the property. Dry fast, photograph everything and keep a log of every call.

Storm water damage questions

Is emergency storm water damage cleanup available in Mission Viejo?

Yes. Call the 24/7 line any hour, including nights and weekends. We offer same-day response when available, and we do not promise an arrival time.

Is storm water considered contaminated?

Usually yes. Runoff picks up soil, bacteria and chemicals on the way in, so it is treated as Category 2 or 3. That affects carpet, pad and drywall.

Can I keep my carpet after a storm leak?

Sometimes. If the water was clean and brief, carpet can be cleaned and dried. The pad underneath is usually removed, and Category 3 water means both come out.

Is rain through my roof a storm water job?

It can be, but roof sources have their own page. See roof leak water damage for tracing and repairing.

Where do I get sandbags in Mission Viejo?

The city offers free sandbags at seven facilities, with a limit of 10 per resident. Ask the city for current locations before you go.

Does homeowners insurance cover storm water?

Often rain entering through a storm-damaged opening may be covered, while flood is usually excluded. Check your policy and ask your insurer.

How long does drying take after a storm?

Usually several days, depending on materials, water category and the area affected. Readings from moisture meters decide when drying ends.
